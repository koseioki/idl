## What are cognitive disabilities?

Cognitive disability is an umbrella term for conditions that affect how the brain processes information.

These conditions can influence how someone thinks, plans, learns, solves problems, and remembers things. 

People may experience cognitive disabilities due to conditions such as:

- Attention deficit disorder (ADD)
- [Attention deficit hyperactivity disorder (ADHD)](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/adhd-adults/)
- [Alzheimer’s disease](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/alzheimers-disease/)
- [Aphasia (language difficulties)](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/aphasia/)
- Asperger’s syndrome
- [Autism](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/autism/)
- [Dementia](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/dementia/)
- [Dyslexia (reading difficulties)](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/dyslexia-in-adults/)
- Intellectual impairment
- Mental illness
- Psychological impairment
- And others <InTextCitation id="10000009" />.


## Barriers people with cognitive disabilities may face on digital devices

People with cognitive disabilities can run into several challenges when using websites, apps, and other digital tools.

Common barriers include:

- Finding important information
- Filling out forms
- Managing passwords
- Understanding information
- Understanding and using controls
- Keeping focused
- Information overload, caused by cluttered design or too many text elements
- Time-outs that cause stress and make it difficult to plan <InTextCitation id="10000010" />

## Cognitive disabilities are diverse

Many conditions involve cognitive disabilities, and within each condition, everyone may experience a different pattern of cognitive difficulties <InTextCitation id="10000011" />.

Therefore, the accessibility needs, requirements, and preferences of people with cognitive disabilities also vary widely <InTextCitation id="10000009" />.

Rather than considering cognitive disabilities as a single category, it is necessary to analyse separately how each specific cognitive difficulty affects a person’s ability to navigate the digital society <InTextCitation id="10000012" />.

## The current guidelines aren't perfect

Professionals have done a lot of work to make the web accessible to people with sensory or motor disabilities.

However, there has been much less work on making the web cognitively accessible, even though there is a growing need for it <InTextCitation id="10000014" />.

[Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG)](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/) are rules that help make websites easier for everyone to use.

WCAG says that:

*“Even content that conforms at the highest level (AAA) will not be accessible to individuals with all types, degrees, or combinations of disability, particularly in the cognitive, language, and learning areas.”* <InTextCitation id="10000013" />

Research has also found that most people with developmental cognitive disabilities had difficulty using websites that followed accessibility guidelines <InTextCitation id="10000014" />.

This shows that following accessibility guidelines does not always mean that a website is easy to use for people with cognitive disabilities.
